Introduction & Problem Statement

Car accidents are one of the most pressing public concerns of contemporary society — and blind corners are where the system is at its most fragile. This chapter frames the problem, poses the research questions, and defines the study's boundaries.

Background of the Study

The global toll of road traffic

Car accidents are considered one of the public concerns in contemporary society (Gonzales et al, 2020; Apeagee & Haaor, 2020) as cited by Faus et al (2023). According to the World Health Organization (2023), approximately 1.19 million people die each year as a result of road traffic incidents — more than half of them pedestrians and motorists. These fatalities occur more often in low- to middle-income countries, which also hold 60% of the world's vehicles.

With society's reliance on vehicles as the mode of transportation, road safety is paramount in preventing fatalities and economic losses — which is why governments create laws, technological products, and programs to lessen the problem.

“Approximately 1.19 million people die each year as a result of road traffic incidents, with more than half being pedestrians and motorists.”
— World Health Organization (2023)

The Philippine context

The Philippines is both a car-centric and a lower-middle income country, which makes it especially vulnerable. The Department of Public Works and Highways (DPWH) and the Automobile Association of the Philippines developed Network-level Safer Roads Investment Plans; the Philippine Road Assessment Program has delivered infrastructure risk assessments for around 4,000 km of roads across 15 regions using the International Road Assessment Programme methodology. The country also issued the National Road Safety Action Plan 2023–2028, targeting a 50% reduction in road fatalities and injuries by 2030.

Yet despite committed partners, legislation progress, and a 20% fatality-reduction commitment, the high incidence of road traffic injuries and fatalities remains a major public health issue — and limited action has been laid out for accidents on streets and blind-spot areas of the road. It is in this context that the researchers developed a vehicle detector using Ultrasonic Distance Sensors (UDS) in blind corners with the goal of achieving better road safety.

Why a diorama? To test the system safely, the researchers built a 1:28-scale physical diorama of a real blind-corner road system — inspired by actual corners on Anabu I and Bayan Luma 3, Imus City, Cavite — with 1:24-scale remote-controlled vehicles as test subjects.

Statement of the Problem

The primary objective of this study is to create a prototype that uses Arduino and ultrasonic distance sensors to prevent accidents and collisions within blind corners by alerting drivers from either side of the road for an approaching vehicle.

To achieve this objective, the study answered the following specific questions:

RQ 1. What is the design of the UDSpot?

RQ 2. How does the UDSpot function as an Arduino system integrated in a road system?

RQ 3. How effective are ultrasonic distance sensors in detecting vehicles?

Hypotheses

The null and alternative hypotheses were formulated as follows:

H₀ · Null hypothesis

“UDSpot is not significantly effective when tested in a simulated environment.”

→
Hₐ · Alternative hypothesis

“UDSpot is significantly effective when tested in a simulated environment.”

Scope and Delimitation

The researchers were honest about the prototype's limits:

UDS sensors do not have the ability to categorize objects. Though they can detect any object in range by sending and receiving ultrasound waves, they cannot determine whether a car or a person is what is being detected.

The illustration board used for the diorama measures only 101.6 cm × 76.2 cm, so it cannot allocate longer road depictions that would further enhance UDSpot's potential — though its size is still enough for UDSpot to work as intended.

UDSpot can fully support the 6 scenarios of one remote-controlled 1:24 vehicle. With two vehicles, it works if they are on the main road, but cannot function as intended if both enter the one-lane, two-way road going in the same direction, one behind the other.

Significance of the Study

Acquiring the Ultrasonic Distance Sensor, this study benefits the following individuals:

Motorists

Improved road safety and traffic management — specifically on one-lane road corners and blind spots — plus the ability to monitor traffic conditions in specific zones for an enhanced driving experience.

Road Administration

Viable knowledge on using technology such as Ultrasonic Distance Sensors as vehicle detection in reducing road accidents.

Road Users & Pedestrians

The installation of a road safety measure such as UDSpot provides safer roads, promoting sustainable community-based road safety systems.

Definition of Terms

In light of the research title, the following terms are utilized and defined operationally:

Stands for Light-Emitting Diode, which can be programmed to turn on when the distance measured by the sensor is equal to a given distance, and turn off when the distance is not equal to the given distance.

A device that uses ultrasonic waves to measure objects in places — such as a car's blind spots — that are hidden from the driver's view.

The process of recognizing and finding vehicles using sensor-based techniques (defined operationally here as UDS detection rather than computer vision).
