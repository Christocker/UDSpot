Discussion

The data has been gathered and the tables are filled. This is where the researchers interpret what the numbers mean — answering each research question with the evidence behind it.

Recap

Three questions, three answers

Each research question was answered through controlled trials in the physical diorama — every figure below is a recorded result, not an estimate.

RQ 1

How effective are ultrasonic distance sensors in detecting vehicles?

Fully effective in the test environment — 12 of 12 detection trials passed.

In Table 1, all four ultrasonic distance sensors detected the passing remote-controlled 1:24-scale vehicles at their specified ranges in all three trials. UDS 1, UDS 3, and UDS 4 detected objects at distances of less than 18 cm, while UDS 2 was calibrated for the 20–37 cm band — the range spanning the left and right lanes of the main road.

This behavior is the natural strength of the sensor: it emits an ultrasound pulse and measures the time the reflected echo takes to return. Any object entering its cone — vehicle or not — produces a reliable echo, which is exactly what a blind corner needs: something is coming, before it is seen. The 100% detection rate confirms the sensors can reliably feed distance data to the Arduino Uno R3 without calibration drift across repeated runs.

RQ 2

Can RGB LEDs provide immediate feedback to drivers?

Yes — 18 of 18 LED output trials matched the expected colors.

Table 2 tested six sensor-to-LED mappings three times each. Every trial produced exactly the expected output — including the flashing (F) states that distinguish “something is approaching now” from the solid ( ~ ) states used for steady conditions:

  • UDS 1 / UDS 2 → main-road lane indicators (LED 3, LED 4): yellow solid or flashing.
  • UDS 3 first trigger → green “go” (LED 1, LED 2) with red “stop” (LED 5).
  • UDS 4 first trigger → red “stop” (LED 1, LED 2) with green “go” (LED 5) — the mirror image.
  • Second triggers flip the pairs to flashing red, warning that a vehicle has fully committed to the one-lane road.

The RGB LEDs mirror the logic of conventional traffic signals — green to proceed, red to yield, flashing to warn — so the feedback is immediate and intuitive, exactly the requirement of the second research question.

RQ 3

Can UDSpot avoid vehicle standoff in a simulated environment?

Yes — 6 of 6 scenarios avoided standoff with the system, vs 2 of 6 without it.

Tables 5 and 6 ran the same six two-vehicle scenarios twice: once with UDSpot active and once with it removed. The comparison in Table 7 tells the story of the blind corner.

Without the system, standoffs occurred in scenarios 2, 4, 5, and 6 — every case where a vehicle was near the turn while the other approached from the other side. Because of the corner, neither driver could see the other in time. The two scenarios that succeeded (1 and 3) only did so because the second vehicle was still far from the turn.

With UDSpot active, all six scenarios — including the four that failed without it — resolved without a standoff. The sensors detected both vehicles and the LED indicators communicated priority to each side before the vehicles met. This result proves that UDSpot is successful in avoiding vehicle standoff between two vehicles in a simulated environment.

“This result proves that the UDSpot system is successful in avoiding vehicle standoff between 2 vehicles, in a simulated environment.” — the researchers

On the hypothesis: because the alternative hypothesis states that UDSpot is significantly effective when tested in a simulated environment — and the standoff trials confirm it — the researchers accepted Ha and rejected H0.

Beyond the numbers

Why the system worked — and where it stops

Strengths observed

  • Cheap, accessible parts — an Arduino Uno R3, four HC-style ultrasonic sensors and five RGB LEDs are widely available.
  • Direction-aware by design — sensor placement per lane lets the system know which road the vehicle is on.
  • Sequential logic — detection order (Table 4) mirrors real traffic priority, so the LED states never contradict each other.
  • Works with existing signals — LED colors follow traffic-light conventions, so learning to read them is instant.
  • Scalable concept — the same Arduino-UDS architecture can grow into real-road systems.

Limitations (scope & delimitation)

  • No object categorization — UDS only measures distance; it cannot tell a car from a person from a wall.
  • Diorama size — the 101.6 × 76.2 cm board cannot hold longer road depictions, limiting long-range scenarios.
  • Scenario ceiling — UDSpot supports the 6 scenarios with one 1:24 vehicle, and two vehicles only while both are on the main road.
  • Two-way one-lane limit — the system cannot function as intended when two vehicles are on the one-lane, two-way road traveling in the same direction, one behind the other.
  • Driver dependent — it only works if drivers know traffic signals, the LEDs are unobstructed, and rules are followed; violations render it ineffective.
