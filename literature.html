Review of Related Literature

Seven bodies of knowledge anchor this study — from the anatomy of Philippine roads to the physics of ultrasonic waves — and each one points to the same conclusion: blind corners are solvable with the right technology.

Reading guide. Each topic below is an expandable summary drawn directly from the paper's review. Use the tabs to switch between literature topics and the traffic-light semantics table the study relied on.

Primary highways in the Philippines are divided into critical arteries — the east-west laterals that cut across the islands, and the north-south backbone that spans from the northernmost point of Luzon to Southern Mindanao. An extensive road network is essential to social mobility and economic activity: it reduces distances between markets, services, and people, and offers access to vital locations for emergency response and regional advancement.

All access routes split off of provincial, city, and national roadways to form secondary roads, which connect provincial capitals and cities to main national routes. Tertiary roads refer to local roads already in place serving specific purposes. The DPWH implemented and oversaw the management of these roads (2014).

According to the Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA) Report of 2021, there has been a worrying trend in the country's road traffic fatalities over the last ten years: a 39% increase, from 7,938 deaths in 2011 to 11,096 deaths.

Car crashes continue to be the leading cause of death for those aged 5–29. Approximately two deaths occur every minute, and over 3,200 deaths occur daily worldwide. The PSA notes that men account for about 84% of these deaths.

People drive mainly using their sense of vision to gather information about obstacles and the path — which makes a blind corner a posing threat, as it makes approaching vehicles “invisible” to drivers. Blind spots around a car that the driver cannot see directly pose risks, especially on one-way roads.

Significant blind spots exist in large vehicles such as trucks, buses, and tractors, which can cause accidents — particularly when turning right and failing to detect cyclists, e-bikes, and pedestrians. Blind zones can also be produced by cargo, headrests, and pillars at the back of the vehicle.

A one-way road is a street intended to accommodate traffic traveling in a single direction, designed to improve traffic flow and safety by directing cars to proceed in a particular direction.

Several technologies exist — and each has a flaw:

  • Convex mirrors: vegetation encroaches on the signs and makes them invisible; they provide a poor image when the mirror's surface is dusty.
  • Geometrical blind-corner models for V2I communications (SISO): significant financial and technical challenges with the V2I channel model.
  • Frontal cameras with built-in prisms: bad visuals in fog or low visibility; fixed to individual vehicles; relatively low setup costs and minimal expertise required.

Incorporating sensors into traffic lights improves pedestrian safety, streamlines traffic, eases congestion, and aids smart-city infrastructure. Yet sensor-timed crossings can be too long or too short depending on crossing density and speed, and pedestrians may still be on the road when vehicles start — the economic cost of pedestrian deaths is predicted to have a 3% impact on world GDP.

UDS are usually seen in places like parking lots and barrier systems. Their projection zone fluctuates with temperature — the zone size increases at lower temperatures and decreases at higher ones. Crucially, ultrasonic sensors can detect vehicles regardless of color and type and compute their speed, making them a reliable solution for sensing approaching vehicles (Pepperl+Fuchs, 2020).

An Arduino Uno is interfaced with the sensor to determine distance; LEDs illuminate and an LCD displays messages if the distance is less than a threshold. The system uses two kinds of indication: a red LED blinks to alert the driver of an approaching vehicle in the blind corner while the green LED stays on.

High-frequency sound waves are emitted by the sensor and reflected back by the object they are aimed at; the sensor measures the distance traveled and the time it takes for the wave to return, based on the speed of sound. Vehicles can also be detected during rainy weather: precipitation affects humidity but has little effect on sound waves due to airborne particles and rain (Mohammed et al., 2020).

Prior work has applied ultrasonic sensing to roads in different contexts:

  • Dhole et al. (2014) proposed a system using an ultrasonic sensor and a camera to improve traffic security and identify vehicles that break traffic rules.
  • HPCL (2017) aimed to reduce accidents on blind corners using radars and speakers that produce a honking sound when an approaching vehicle is detected.
  • Vishnu & Manjesha (2020) built a system to aid motorists on blind corners — implemented in a diorama with ultrasonic sensors, LEDs, Arduino Uno ATmega328P, Piezo, and LCDs.
  • Atombo et al. (2020) used similar components but with a more detailed evaluation process.

Despite similarities in idea and components, this study focuses on a different context and road design: road systems with one-lane, two-way roads with blind corners.

ADDIE is a five-stage development process — Analysis, Design, Development, Implementation, and Evaluation — executed incrementally to achieve a project's goal efficiently and in a planned way. Critically, the ADDIE model advocates a reflective and iterative process so past mistakes can be avoided and corrected in future cycles (O'Neill, 2024).

Yu et al. (2021) developed an intelligent virtual reality interactive system based on the ADDIE model for pour-over coffee brewing machines — results showed it matched participants' learning requirements. Amish (2023) built a virtual laboratory using ADDIE and found it effective for interactive learning.

Standard traffic lights

The semantics that UDSpot's LED signals are modeled after.

Red

Total stop

A red signal indicates a total stop. Hold your position until the light turns green — running a red light is unlawful and extremely dangerous.

Yellow

Prepare to stop

When there is a yellow light, the green light is soon to end — stop unless it is not safe to continue.

Green

Go

All a green light indicates is “GO.” Allow cars, bicycles, and pedestrians using the intersection to pass before you leave.

Flashing red

Stop & yield

Signals you to stop and observe right-of-way laws — consider it a “STOP” signal and proceed appropriately.

Flashing yellow

Proceed with caution

Proceed cautiously and slowly; it alerts cars to potentially dangerous situations ahead, like a school crossing.

Arrows

Directional signals

Red, yellow, flashing-yellow, and green arrows govern turns — wait for the green arrow, or yield carefully when the yellow arrow flashes.

From the literature, the researchers synthesized the following:

  • Literature on roads and highways in the Philippines emphasizes how important road safety measures are to economic growth, social mobility, and disaster preparedness.
  • Car crashes continue to be a major cause of death, especially among young people; the Philippines has seen an alarming rise in road-crash deaths over the past ten years.
  • On Philippine roadways, blind spots and bends pose serious safety hazards because they make it more difficult for drivers to see approaching cars and pedestrians.
  • Road safety initiatives — particularly UDS and the integration of sensors — show promise for improving road safety and lowering accident rates: UDS can identify oncoming cars of any color or type, giving drivers real-time alerts.
  • The problem is complex, and solving it requires a comprehensive strategy — the creation of safer, more sustainable road safety measures like UDSpot, following the affordances of the ADDIE model, can provide a safer road for all.
